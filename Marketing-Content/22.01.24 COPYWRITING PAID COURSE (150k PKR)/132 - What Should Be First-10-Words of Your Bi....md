# What Should Be the First 10 Words of Your Bio?

## 💡 Tóm Tắt Cốt Lõi (Key Takeaways)
- The first 10 words of your bio are the only part visible without clicking "more," making them decisive for immediate attention.
- Starting bios with "I, me, my" or listing degrees/experience backfires because audiences prioritize their own needs and problems over the creator's background.
- Effective bio openings lead with client value, a relatable pain point, or a benefit-driven proposition to spark interest instantly.

## 📖 Nội Dung Chi Tiết
### 1. Bio Visibility & The 10-Word Rule
- Social media platforms typically truncate bios after a few lines or a set character count visible in the preview.
- The human eye scans quickly; if the first 10 words don't resonate, the reader scrolls past.
- This section of the lesson emphasizes that "attention is the prerequisite for interest"—without it, the rest of the bio matters little.
- Key principle: Every word in the first 10 must serve the goal of grabbing attention, not self-praise.

### 2. Common Mistake: The "I, Me, My" Trap
- Many creators default to intros like "I have this degree and I have this experience," thinking credentials build immediate trust.
- The analysis shows this approach fails because it centers the creator, not the audience.
- Audiences care about: "What's in it for me?" "Can this person solve my problem?" immediately.
- Correction strategy: Replace self-intros with value-first statements. Example: Instead of "I am a certified graphic designer," use "I help brands stand out with unforgettable logos."

### 3. The Attention-Interest Framework
- **Step 1 – Get Attention:** The first 10 words must contain a hook, a question, or a bold claim targeting the ideal client.
- **Step 2 – Build Interest:** Once attention is secured, the remaining bio lines (and profile content) nurture curiosity and desire.
- **Step 3 – Action:** Clear direction or social proof follows naturally.
- The lesson reinforces: "If you don't get attention, you can't build maximum interest, no matter how good the rest is."

> **Ví dụ / Case Study:** 
- Before: "I have 10 years of experience in marketing and I've worked with big brands." (Self-focused, easily skipped)
- After: "Struggling to get clients? Discover the 3-step system I use." (Client pain point first, attention-grabbing)
- This reorientation shifts the reader's mindset from "who is this?" to "how can this help me?"

### 4. Structural Blueprint for a High-Converting Bio
- **Profile Picture:** Clear, professional, face or logo (visual anchor).
- **Title:** Role or niche descriptor (e.g., "Brand Strategist | Copywriter").
- **First 10 Words:** Value proposition or pain-agitate-solution hook.
- **Remaining Lines:** Credentials, social proof, call-to-action (visible only after "more" click, but support the initial hook).
- Bold key terms: *Hook, Value Proposition, Pain Point, Attention Span, Truncation*.

## ❓ Câu Hỏi & Trả Lời Trọng Tâm (Q&A for Search)
- **Hỏi:** How to write the first 10 words of a social media bio to attract customers?
  - **Đáp:** Focus on the client's pain point or value rather than self-introduction. Start with a compelling question or benefit-driven statement, e.g., "Struggling to get clients?" instead of "I have 10 years of experience." The first 10 words must grab attention immediately since only a small portion is visible before clicking "More".
- **Hỏi:** Why is starting a bio with "I, me, my" ineffective?
  - **Đáp:** Audiences care about their own needs and problems first, not the creator's background initially. Bios beginning with "I have this degree..." or "I have this experience..." often cause readers to scroll past because they aren't immediately relevant. Shifting from "I" to "you/the client" is key to retaining attention.
- **Hỏi:** What is the process to convert attention into interest in a bio?
  - **Đáp:** Step 1: The first 10 words create an attention hook (question, challenge, or benefit promise). Step 2: Subsequent lines (even if truncated initially) support with credentials, social proof, or a call to action. Step 3: Link to external content or next steps. Attention is the prerequisite; if the first step is missed, later steps cannot compensate.
