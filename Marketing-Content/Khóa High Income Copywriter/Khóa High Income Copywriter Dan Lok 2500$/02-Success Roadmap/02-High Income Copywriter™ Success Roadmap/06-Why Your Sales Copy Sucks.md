# Why Your Sales Copy Sucks

## 💡 Tóm Tắt Cốt Lõi (Key Takeaways)
- 95% of sales copy fails because it's ego-centric ("me, me, me") rather than customer-focused.
- Customers don't care about you or your business; they care about their own needs, problems, and desires.
- Effective copy is conversational, written as you speak, and doesn't need to be formal proper English.
- The "cheap copywriting mindset" prioritizes the seller's ego over the client's perspective.
- The golden rule: "You are not your customer"—expert copywriters must shift perspective to the buyer.

## 📖 Nội Dung Chi Tiết
### 1. Why 95% of Sales Copy Is Crap
- **Ego vs Client-Ego:** Most entrepreneurs write about themselves ("I've been in business for 15 years, here's why we're great"), which centers on their own ego. Successful copy shifts focus entirely to the client's ego, needs, and problems.
- **"Me, Me, Me" Trap:** Copy overloaded with company history, achievements, and self-praise fails because customers are primarily concerned with themselves.
- **Conversational Tone Is Okay:** You write the way you speak. Sometimes imperfect English is fine; the goal is to be conversational and relatable, not grammatically perfect.
- **Two Core Issues:** (1) A cheap copywriting mindset that values the seller's perspective, and (2) Writing about the self instead of the customer's world.

> **Ví dụ / Case Study:** A common pattern is the "about us" section that boasts company history and awards. When shifted to addressing the customer's specific pain point with a solution-oriented narrative, conversion rates typically improve significantly.

### 2. The Golden Rule: "You Are Not Your Customer"
- The mantra "You are not your customer" is the foundation of effective copy.
- Your personal preferences, tastes, and business perspective are not your target audience's.
- Clients often resist removing "complicated language" or "talking about ourselves," but data shows customer-centric copy converts better.
- Expertise matters: just because a client pays doesn't mean they know what they're doing regarding copy psychology.

### 3. Working with Clients: Results Over Design
- **Design vs Results:** Clarify whether the client is paying for aesthetic design or measurable results. This distinction guides copy decisions.
- **Testing Over Arguing:** When clients resist customer-focused copy, the best approach is not to argue but to test. Use tools like Google Analytics to run A/B tests.
- **Data-Driven Validation:** It's easy now to set up tracking. If a test shows a 20% conversion rate with customer-centric copy vs 10% with ego-driven copy, the data speaks.
- **Client Education:** Part of your expertise is educating clients that just because they give money doesn't make them right, and it doesn't mean they know what they're doing. Your job is to guide them with proven principles.

## ❓ Câu Hỏi & Trả Lời Trọng Tâm (Q&A for Search)
- **Hỏi:** Why does most sales copy fail?
  - **Đáp:** Most sales copy fails because it's ego-centric, focusing on the seller's achievements, history, and "me, me, me" narrative rather than the customer's needs, problems, and desires. Customers care about themselves, not the seller.
- **Hỏi:** What is the golden rule of effective copywriting?
  - **Đáp:** The golden rule is: "You are not your customer." Effective copy must be written from the customer's perspective, not the writer's or business owner's. Shifting focus from self to customer is the key to conversion.
- **Hỏi:** How should a copywriter handle client resistance to removing self-focused language?
  - **Đáp:** Focus on results, not design preferences. Instead of arguing, propose testing the customer-centric version using analytics (e.g., Google Analytics, conversion tracking). Data is the objective arbiter; if the new copy converts better, the client typically agrees. Expertise exists to guide the client, and just because they pay doesn't mean they know what they're doing regarding copy psychology.
